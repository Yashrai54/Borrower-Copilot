1. FOIR - Fixed Obligation to income ratio : metric used by banks to check how much of your income goes toward paying existing debts.
(Total Monthly Fixed Obligations ÷ Gross Monthly Income) × 100

Fixed Obligations : Existing Loan EMIs, credit card minimum payments, sometimes rent or insurance.
Monthly Income: Salary, business income, rental income before tax deductions

No RBI mandated threshold for FOIR. 50% maximum only for Microfinance loans.

Lending practices

(i). lenders cannot charge full-month interest rate for mid-month repayments, the interest are calculated exactly for the  period which is outstanding 
(ii). Interest  from the day of disbursment not from sanction
(iii) lenders must tell APR, processing fee or any upfront charges before loan execution.


2. EMI (Equated monthly installments): fixed payment amount borrower pays to lender monthly until the loan is paid off.

Principal: The actual amount of money you borrowed.
Interest: The extra fee the lender charges you for borrowing the money.

EMI = P*R*(1+R)^N/ (1+R)^N -1

3. APR (Annual Percentage rate ): Yearly cost of borrowing money or earning on an investment, expressed as a percentage.

Nominal Interest Rate: The base cost of borrowing the principal amount.

Additional Fees: Upfront charges like processing fees, administrative costs, or mandatory insurance tied to the loan.

Exclusions: Simple APR typically does not factor in the impact of compound interest over time.

4. Credit Score

A CIBIL score is a three-digit number from 300 to 900 that shows how likely you are to pay back a loan.

Payment History: Paying your credit card bills and loan EMIs on time.Credit Utilization: How much of your available credit limit you use.Credit Mix: Having a healthy balance of secured (like home loans) and unsecured (like credit cards) debt.Credit Enquiries: Applying for too many loans or cards in a short time.

Disposable income is the money left over from an individual's or household's gross earnings after paying taxes and mandatory charges.
Discretionary Income: The remainder of disposable income after paying for essential needs like housing, utilities, and food.

Net Income: Often called take-home pay, this is your total earnings (gross income) minus all payroll deductions, such as income taxes, social security, health insurance premiums, and retirement contributions (like a 401(k)).

Net Income can be a better measure from borrowers end compared to gross income

An emergency buffer is a separate pool of easily accessible cash saved strictly to pay for unexpected life crises, urgent bills, or sudden job loss. It is also commonly called an emergency fund or cash safety net. 

Existing debt means any money or financial obligation that a person, company, or government currently owes to a lender.

A standard car loan is a secured loan because the vehicle you buy acts as collateral.
A business loan is money borrowed from a bank or financial group to help start, run, or grow a company. (Unsecured)
A loan against property (LAP) is a secured loan where you pledge a residential, commercial, or industrial property as collateral to borrow funds while keeping ownership of the asset.
